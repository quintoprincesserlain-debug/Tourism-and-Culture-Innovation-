# Task 5: Analysis and Diagnosis

## Innovation sector
Tourism and culture.

## Problem diagnosis
Travelers may struggle to find trustworthy information about community-led cultural experiences, while local guides, artisans, and cultural organizations often have limited digital visibility. At the same time, tourism can place pressure on heritage and natural sites if visitors are not informed about local customs, capacity, and responsible behavior.

## Root causes
- Information about experiences is spread across different platforms.
- Small providers may have limited marketing budgets or digital skills.
- Cultural interpretation may be incomplete, inaccurate, or created without community input.
- Destination promotion may focus on visitor numbers rather than community well-being and preservation.

## Proposed response
Develop a mobile-friendly cultural tourism guide that lists verified heritage experiences, local guides, festivals, crafts, and food traditions. Add concise cultural context, accessibility details, responsible-travel guidance, and direct contact or booking information where available.

## Intended users
- Domestic and international travelers
- Community guides and small tourism businesses
- Artists, artisans, and cultural organizations
- Local governments and heritage managers

## Expected outcomes
- Better discovery of local experiences
- Increased opportunities for community-based livelihoods
- Improved visitor understanding of local heritage
- More responsible visitor behavior
- Stronger community participation in cultural representation

## Risks and safeguards
- **Misrepresentation:** community review and consent for content.
- **Overtourism:** capacity guidance and promotion of alternatives.
- **Digital exclusion:** simple tools and offline-friendly information where possible.
- **Privacy:** minimal data collection and clear consent.
- **Inaccessibility:** test with users with varied access needs and publish verified details.

## Evaluation indicators
1. Number of active local providers and community partners.
2. Visitor engagement and satisfaction.
3. Inquiries or bookings directed to local providers.
4. Feedback from cultural practitioners and residents.
5. Accessibility and content accuracy issues resolved.
6. Evidence of responsible visitor behavior and heritage protection.

## Conclusion
Tourism and culture can be innovative when digital tools strengthen, rather than replace, local knowledge and community leadership. The project should begin with a small pilot, use community-approved information, measure both economic and cultural outcomes, and improve based on feedback.
